Single-Source Provable Fairness in Fe

FairDice compiles one pure Fe function (src/outcome.fe) to both EVM bytecode on Base Sepolia and a WebAssembly verifier running in your browser.

1. Why This Is Impossible in Solidity

Solidity compiles only to EVM bytecode. Every off-chain verifier for a Solidity contract has to re-implement the contract's math in a second language (JavaScript, Python, or Rust), creating a permanent drift risk between what runs on-chain and what the verifier checks.

Fe 26.7.0 compiles the exact same storage-free, context-free module to both targets: 

src/outcome.fe  (pure Fe: roll, compute_payout, max_payout)
  ├── fe build --backend sonatina  ──▶  out/FairDice.bin   (Base Sepolia EVM)
  └── fe build --backend wasm      ──▶  out/feguard.wasm   (Browser + CLI verifier)

When you click Verify on the Play page, your browser executes the house's own compiled roll() and compute_payout() functions directly in WebAssembly — zero JavaScript re-implementation.

2. Payout Formula & House Edge

Players pick a roll-under target between 1 and 95. Outcomes lie uniformly in 0..99 via keccak_words([entropy_value, client_commitment, bet_id]) % 100. A bet wins when outcome < target, paying floor(wager * 98 / target). At target = 49, win probability is 49% and payout is an exact 2.00x, giving an explicit 2% house edge (98% RTP before integer wei truncation).

3. Why We Replaced Commit–Reveal With Pyth Entropy V2

Our initial commit–reveal deployment (0xed5d38e1…326b) had a structural flaw that we documented against ourselves in reports/finding-fairdice-withholding.md: because only the house held server_seed, the house could decline winning outcomes by staying silent until the 256-block refund window returned only the player's wager. No penalty constant fixes this — a small penalty is cheaper than paying a 98x win, and a max-payout penalty is farmable by the player withholding their own client_seed.

The current contract (0xd48ed9b0…c774) eliminates secret server seeds completely by requesting randomness from Pyth Entropy V2 (0x41c9e39574F40Ad34c79f1C99B66A45eFB830d4c). Neither player nor house can withhold the callback, and refundExpired (256 blocks) serves strictly as a provider-failure escape hatch.
